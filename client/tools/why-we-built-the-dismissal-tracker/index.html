What the Dismissal Tracker Does, and Why We Built It

The Dismissal Tracker is a free, no-account tool for keeping a plain, dated record of appointments where a symptom you reported didn't get the response you expected. It isn't a diagnostic tool, and it isn't built to be used against anyone — it exists because this experience is widely reported, and there weren't many purpose-built ways to keep track of it.

What it actually records

Each entry captures five things: the date, the clinic or practice name, the clinician's name (optional), which symptoms you reported, and what was said in response — plus whether it was later resolved. That's it. It doesn't score your symptoms, suggest a diagnosis, or draft a complaint. It's closer to a personal appointment log than a clinical instrument, built to be easy to fill in right after a visit while the details are still fresh.

What it deliberately doesn't do

It doesn't judge whether a given appointment counts as "dismissal" — that's a call only you can make about your own experience, and the tool doesn't attempt it for you. It also isn't framed as evidence against a clinician; most of what gets described as dismissal traces back to short appointment slots and gaps in perimenopause-specific training, not individual malice, and the record is meant to make your next conversation clearer, not to build a case.

Why we built it

Feeling dismissed during a perimenopause appointment is a widely reported experience, but purpose-built tools for keeping track of it are hard to find — most symptom trackers have a general notes field at best, which isn't structured for this specific purpose. We built a dedicated tool because the fields that actually matter here (date, clinic, what was reported, what was said back, and resolution status) are different from a general symptom note, and having them in a consistent structure makes the resulting record more useful to bring into a future appointment.

How it protects your privacy

The free version of this tool runs entirely in your browser — nothing you enter is sent to a server. If you use it inside the full Ripple app to keep records over time, entries are protected by Ripple's zero-knowledge vault architecture: data is encrypted on your own device, using a key derived from your PIN or device, before it's ever saved. Ripple's servers never hold a readable copy of your dismissal records, symptom logs, or cycle data — not even Ripple can read them.

Why cycle change matters more than any single symptom

Hot flashes, sleep trouble, and mood changes get most of the attention when people talk about perimenopause — and Ripple's own Greene Climacteric Scale measures exactly those. But the STRAW+10 consensus staging framework, the framework researchers and clinicians use to describe where someone is in the menopause transition, is built primarily on menstrual bleeding patterns, not symptom severity. Persistent cycle-length variability, gaps of 60 days or more, and 12 consecutive months without a period are the markers that actually define each stage. This tool exists to cover that gap directly.

What this tool is, and what it isn't

The Cycle Change Tracker is original content written by Ripple, grounded in the STRAW+10 consensus framework — it is not a translation, adaptation, or reproduction of any licensed instrument, and it carries no licensing restriction. It is also not a scored or validated instrument: there's no total, no cutoff, and no severity number anywhere on this page. It produces a plain-language pattern description based on the timing you describe, in this priority order — a 12-month absence of periods first, then a 60-day-or-longer gap, then repeated cycle-length shifts of a week or more, then a single such shift, and otherwise no cycle marker detected. Flow, duration, and predictability are reflected back descriptively because they're common real experiences during the transition, but they don't change which pattern you're matched to.

If you're using hormonal contraception or a hormonal IUD

The first question asks about this directly, and it's a hard gate, not a suggestion: hormonal contraception, a hormonal IUD, and some other treatments change bleeding patterns on their own, which makes cycle timing an unreliable — sometimes meaningless — signal for staging while you're using them. Answering "Yes" skips the pattern classification entirely rather than producing a result that looks informative but isn't. Symptom-based tools like the Greene Climacteric Scale, and preparing for a direct conversation with a clinician, are more useful starting points in that situation.

Patterns worth raising with a clinician promptly

One question on this page stands apart from the rest: spotting between periods, bleeding after sex, any bleeding after 12 or more months without a period, and unusually heavy bleeding. These carry no scoring weight and don't feed into the pattern above — they're checked independently, and they apply whether or not the hormonal-treatment question gated your result. Flagging any of them surfaces a note recommending you raise it with a clinician sooner rather than later. That's a routine, sensible step for these specific patterns — not a sign that anything is necessarily wrong, and not a substitute for a full clinical evaluation.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Cycle Change Tracker?

The Cycle Change Tracker is a free, original tool from Ripple that asks 8 quick questions about your menstrual cycle — length variability, skipped cycles, flow, duration, and predictability — and matches your answers to a menopause-transition pattern based on the STRAW+10 staging framework. It produces a pattern description, not a score or a diagnosis.

Is this the same kind of tool as the Greene Climacteric Scale?

No. The Greene Climacteric Scale is a validated, licensed research instrument that scores symptom severity. The Cycle Change Tracker is original content written by Ripple, grounded in the STRAW+10 consensus staging criteria, and it never produces a number or a total — only a plain-language pattern description. The two tools are meant to complement each other, not replace one another.

What is STRAW+10?

STRAW+10 (Stages of Reproductive Aging Workshop +10) is a consensus staging framework, published by Harlow and colleagues in 2012, that describes the menopause transition in stages using menstrual bleeding patterns as the primary marker — things like persistent cycle-length variability, 60-day-or-longer gaps between periods, and 12 consecutive months without a period.

Does this tool diagnose menopause or perimenopause?

No. It describes which STRAW+10-informed bleeding pattern your answers most closely match. It doesn't diagnose perimenopause or menopause, estimate how much time is left before your final period, or replace a conversation with a clinician about your specific situation.

What if I'm using hormonal birth control or a hormonal IUD?

The first question asks about this directly, because hormonal contraception and hormonal IUDs change bleeding patterns on their own — that makes cycle timing an unreliable signal for staging while you're using them. If you answer yes, the tool skips pattern classification entirely rather than giving you a misleading result, and points you to symptom-based tools instead.

Why does one question ask about bleeding after sex or between periods?

That question exists separately from the pattern classification and carries no scoring weight. Some bleeding patterns — spotting between periods, bleeding after sex, any bleeding after 12 or more months without a period, or unusually heavy bleeding — are usually worth a clinician's attention promptly rather than watched over time, regardless of where you are in the transition. Selecting any of these surfaces a note about that; it isn't a diagnosis of anything serious, just a prompt to raise it.

Does this tool store or send my answers anywhere?

No. Every answer and every classification is calculated in your browser using JavaScript running on your own device. Nothing is transmitted to Ripple's servers, stored, or logged, and this tool doesn't save your answers between visits — refreshing the page clears everything, since there's no vault or history storage in this version.

What does Ripple do with the Cycle Change Tracker?

Nothing, on this page — your answers stay on your device unless you print or save your results yourself. This free tool doesn't require an account to use.
